# Client-style memo: Queensland operating-window example

**Question.** For a hypothetical Queensland business, would moving a 5 kW activity lasting two hours from 16:00–18:00 to 11:00–13:00 have reduced historical wholesale-price exposure? The background profile is 5 kW from 08:00–20:00. Either schedule consumes 70 kWh per day. These figures are demonstration assumptions; they do not represent a real business or Coles.

**Finding.** Over the reserved 14 June–12 September 2026 period, 75 of 91 days had a complete set of AEMO prices marked `FIRM`. The alternative schedule had lower calculated wholesale exposure on all 75 of those days. Across those paired days, the total was **$236.43 under the alternative versus $291.74 under the original schedule**, a difference of **$55.31 (18.96%)**. The median daily difference was $0.73; the smallest was $0.18. The calculation was independently checked with a SQL sum of the two fixed price windows and matched to the cent.

**Coverage sensitivity.** Sixteen holdout days were excluded from the primary comparison because one or more archived prices carried AEMO's `NOT FIRM` status. Including those flagged intervals as an explicitly provisional sensitivity allows all 91 days to be compared: $291.49 alternative versus $357.24 original, a $65.75 difference (18.41%). The direction is unchanged, but these additional prices may have been subject to review. Across the earlier development period, 150 days had complete firm prices; the alternative was cheaper on 97.3% of those days and had a worst daily difference of **−$0.94**. This shows the pattern was not universal across the broader year.

**Recommendation.** The historical pattern is strong enough to justify a customer-specific feasibility study. Before changing operations, obtain the business's interval meter readings, actual tariff or wholesale-linked contract, constraints on moving the activity, and the cost of rescheduling work. Recalculate using those inputs and assess how much of the historical difference could reach the customer's bill. A fixed retail tariff may pass none of the interval-by-interval wholesale benefit through to the customer.

**Interpretation.** These are retrospective wholesale-price calculations. They are not a price forecast, a retail-bill estimate, or a guarantee of future savings. The holdout spans winter and early spring; future seasons and exceptional market events may differ. Negative prices and price spikes were retained. Incomplete days were excluded from both schedules equally. The data, assumptions, excluded dates, and daily paired results are recorded in `holdout_result.json` and `holdout_sensitivity.json`.
